Marketplace functionality



Adding a rating system is a brilliant idea for a marketplace, but because KoboPoll is a survey platform, a standard 5-star rating might actually hurt your business model if not executed carefully.

Here is a candid breakdown of how you should handle ratings for brands, balancing the Fiverr vibe with the realities of market research.

The Problem with a Standard 5-Star System
If you let users rate brands out of 5 stars (like Fiverr), you run into a major risk: Review Bombing.
If a user gets disqualified halfway through a survey because they don't fit the demographic (e.g., the brand only wants people who own a car, and the user selects "No"), that user might get angry and leave a 1-star review. If a brand gets a low rating, they will simply take their money to a different survey platform.

What You Should Do Instead (The Upwork Approach)
Instead of a subjective 5-star rating, use Objective Trust Metrics on the survey cards. This builds immense trust with the earners without punishing the brands for being strict with their data.

Here is what you should display on the survey cards instead of stars:

Payment Verified Badge (✅): A green checkmark showing that the brand has already deposited the funds into KoboPoll's escrow. This guarantees the earner that the money is real and ready to be paid.

Approval Rate (e.g., 98% Approval): Shows what percentage of completed surveys this brand actually accepts and pays out for. If a brand routinely rejects responses to avoid paying, this number drops, warning users away.

Average Time to Pay (e.g., Pays in 2 Hours): Indicates how fast the brand reviews and approves the data. Earners love fast cash, so this creates healthy competition among brands to approve data quickly.

How this looks on a Survey Card:
Card Example:
Title: Mobile Banking Habits in Lagos
By: FinTech Startup (✅ Payment Verified)
Reward: ₦500 | Time: 3 Mins | Slots Left: 45/100
📊 Brand Stats: 95% Approval Rate | ⚡ Pays in < 24 Hours

When to use a Star Rating
If you still want a star rating, flip it. Have the users rate the Survey Experience out of 5 stars, not the brand itself.

Was the survey glitchy?

Were the questions easy to understand?

Was the estimated time accurate?

This gives you (the platform owner) valuable feedback on which brands are building terrible surveys, so you can help them improve their forms to get better data.
